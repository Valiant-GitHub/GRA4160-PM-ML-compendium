Understanding Supervised Learning
▶Supervised learning: Like a student learning from a solved exercise, it predicts new
outcomes based on learned patterns.
▶It involves input-output pair training, like predicting house prices from their size and
location.
▶Key algorithms include:
▶Linear regression: For continuous output prediction.
▶k-nearest neighbors: Classiﬁes based on similarity.
▶Decision trees: Maps decisions and their possible consequences.
▶Neural networks: For complex pattern recognition.

===== [PAGE 11] =====
Bias-Variance Trade-oﬀ
▶A core concept in ML: Bias is how much your predictions systematically deviate from
the true values.
▶Variance is how sensitive your model is to ﬂuctuations in the training set.
▶High bias models:
▶Underﬁt the data (oversimpliﬁed).
▶Have consistent but inaccurate predictions.
▶High variance models:
▶Overﬁt the data (too complex).
▶Perform well on training data but poorly on new data.
▶Goal: Find a balance that minimizes overall error.

▶Useful for inference: helps understand how diﬀerent features inﬂuence outcomes.

Parametric vs. Non-Parametric Models
▶Parametric models (e.g., linear regression):
▶Assume a speciﬁc form for the relationship between features and output.
▶Have a ﬁxed number of parameters to learn.
▶Fast to train and interpret but can be misleading if the assumed form is incorrect.
▶Non-parametric models (e.g., kNN):
▶Make fewer assumptions about data distribution.
▶Number of parameters can grow with the dataset.
▶Flexible but often more computationally expensive and can overﬁt without careful tuning.
